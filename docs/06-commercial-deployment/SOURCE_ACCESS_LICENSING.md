# Source Access and Licensing Model

## Ownership statement

Recommended contract concept:

> The customer owns its data and deployment environment. The vendor retains ownership of the core software and intellectual property. The customer receives a non-exclusive, non-transferable internal-use licence according to the purchased tier.

## Rights by asset

| Asset | Recommended position |
|---|---|
| Core platform | Vendor owned |
| Generic connector framework | Vendor owned |
| Generic product improvements | Vendor owned |
| Customer data | Customer owned |
| Customer confidential source material | Customer confidential |
| Customer branding | Customer owned or licensed to customer |
| Product configuration | Customer use rights; reusable schema remains vendor IP |
| Reusable generic templates | Vendor owned |
| Bespoke integration | Defined in statement of work |
| Product source code | Licensed, not assigned |
| Right to host for third parties | Prohibited unless partner agreement |
| Right to resell | Prohibited unless partner agreement |

## Standard source-access restrictions

- Internal use only
- No redistribution
- No external managed service
- No resale
- No publication
- No removal of notices
- No use to create a competing product
- No disclosure beyond authorized employees and contractors
- Customer modifications clearly marked
- Support excludes unreviewed modifications
- Security fixes may require upgrade to a supported version

## Customer modifications

Recommended policy:

- Customer may propose changes.
- Vendor may review and merge reusable improvements.
- Customer-specific changes should use extension points or configuration.
- Direct core modifications create a customer-maintained fork and are outside standard support unless contracted.
- Vendor is not required to merge changes that weaken security, portability or product direction.

## Continuity

For customers concerned about vendor continuity, prefer source escrow over routine unrestricted source distribution.

## Legal review

This document is a product position, not final legal wording. A software-licensing lawyer must prepare the final EULA, source-access terms, escrow terms, IP clauses, warranty, liability and data-processing terms.
