# Market and Competitor Review

**Review date:** 2026-09-05  
**Status:** Initial product research, not a market forecast

## Market problem hypothesis

The target customer already has project data but still relies on PMs or PMO staff to:

- Request missing updates
- Reconcile Jira and spreadsheet data
- Prepare leadership reports
- Explain project delays
- Follow up on blockers and dependencies
- Repeat information across systems

The buying problem is not only report generation. It is the recurring cost and risk of keeping delivery information current, accountable and decision-ready.

## Competitor categories

### Native project-tool AI

Examples:

- Atlassian Rovo
- ClickUp Brain and agents
- monday AI
- Asana Smart Status
- Linear update reminders and summaries

Strengths:

- Deep native access
- Low setup for existing customers
- Built-in permissions
- Rapid feature development

Implication:

“AI-generated status report” or “agent updates Jira” is not sufficient differentiation.

### Cross-tool reporting

Examples:

- Screenful
- Integration and dashboard products
- BI platforms with PM connectors

Strengths:

- Multi-tool aggregation
- Scheduled reports
- Dashboards

Implication:

Multi-tool reporting alone is not sufficient differentiation.

### PPM platforms

Examples:

- PPM Express
- Planview
- ServiceNow Strategic Portfolio Management
- Clarity
- OnePlan
- Cora

Strengths:

- Portfolio hierarchy
- Resource and financial planning
- Strategic alignment
- Enterprise governance

Implication:

The product should not attempt to become a full PPM platform in the initial releases.

### Workflow automation platforms

Examples:

- n8n
- Activepieces
- Power Automate
- Zapier
- Make

Strengths:

- Large integration catalogs
- Flexible workflows
- Rapid prototypes

Implication:

These platforms can reproduce isolated reminder or reporting flows. The product moat must be the PMO-specific evidence, policy, source authority, contradiction, approval and explanation model.

## Proposed market position

> A customer-hosted project delivery assurance agent that keeps project information current, follows up on missing commitments, recommends interventions, controls source-system updates and gives leadership evidence-backed answers across existing tools.

## Differentiation package

The product is differentiated only when these capabilities work together:

1. Field-level Source Authority Matrix
2. Project Fact and Evidence Ledger
3. Current versus stale versus conflicting classification
4. Contextual update collection
5. Configurable reminder and escalation
6. Deterministic delivery signals
7. Role-specific advice
8. Approval diff
9. Controlled write-back and action receipts
10. Leadership Q&A with claim-level evidence
11. Customer-hosted and BYOK deployment
12. Shadow mode

## Target customer profile

Strong fit:

- 15 to 200 active projects
- Jira plus spreadsheets or multiple PM tools
- Recurring weekly or monthly reporting
- PMO or delivery-operations function
- Significant manual update chasing
- Security or data-control concerns
- Need for management or customer status packs

Weak fit:

- Fewer than five simple projects
- One tool fully satisfies reporting and coordination needs
- Customer expects full financial/resource PPM immediately
- No willingness to provide data or participate in configuration
- Primary requirement is employee monitoring

## Competitive response to “Why not Rovo?”

Recommended answer:

> Native Atlassian AI is valuable for Jira and Confluence work. This product implements the organisation’s governed PMO operating process across systems. It adds field-level source authority, update accountability, human confirmation, cross-source contradiction handling, action receipts, formal reporting and customer-controlled deployment.

Do not claim that native tools cannot summarize, act or access external sources.

## Validation gates

Green:

- Ten or more substantive PMO/delivery conversations
- Five follow-up demonstrations
- Three real reporting samples
- Two design partners
- One paid pilot or signed commercial commitment

Yellow:

- Positive interest but no data, sponsor or paid pilot

Red:

- Target customers consistently state that native tools fully solve the problem and reject the proposed evidence, governance and hosting differentiation

## Open questions

- Is customer hosting a purchase driver or only a stated preference?

- Which leadership questions occur frequently enough to justify product use?
- What is the minimum useful spreadsheet mapping?
